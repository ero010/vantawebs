How to Remove GPS Location from iPhone Photos

iPhone photos contain GPS coordinates by default. Every photo you take includes your exact latitude, longitude, and sometimes altitude embedded in the EXIF data. When you share these photos on social media, the platforms usually strip this data. But when you share via email, messaging apps, Discord, or AirDrop — the GPS coordinates travel with the file.

This means someone could extract your home address, workplace, or frequented locations just from a photo you sent in a group chat. It's a significant privacy risk that most people don't think about.

You can check whether a photo has location data in seconds. Open the Photos app, open the photo, and swipe up. If you see a map with a pin under the photo, that image has GPS coordinates saved in it. On a Mac, open the photo in Preview and press Cmd+I, then look at the GPS tab. If there are numbers for latitude and longitude, anyone you send the original file to can see them too.

How to remove location from a single photo on iPhone (iOS 15 and later). Apple added a built-in option for this, and it works well for one-off shares. Open Photos, open the picture, tap the ... / Adjust button or swipe up to reveal details, tap the map or location line, then choose No Location or adjust the location. On newer iOS versions this is under the info panel: swipe up on the photo, tap the map thumbnail, then tap No Location. This creates a version without GPS, but it keeps the rest of the metadata like date and camera settings.

How to share without location. When you tap Share, tap Options at the top of the share sheet and turn off Location. Then send that copy. This is handy when you want to text someone a photo but do not want to change the original in your library. Just remember you have to do it every time — iPhone turns Location back on for the next share by default.

How to remove location from several photos at once. Open Photos, tap Select, pick the photos, tap the ... button, then look for location or adjust options. On Mac, select multiple photos in the Photos app, then use Image → Location → Remove Location. If you have hundreds of vacation photos to clean before posting them somewhere that keeps metadata, doing it one by one gets tedious. How to stop iPhone saving location in future photos. Go to Settings → Privacy & Security → Location Services → Camera and set it to Never. New photos will no longer have GPS. Keep in mind this only affects future shots. Every photo you already took still has its coordinates until you strip them. Also, if you use a third-party camera app, check its own location permission — it may have a separate setting.

What gets stripped vs what survives

Using the iPhone No Location option removes GPS coordinates, but it leaves the date taken, iPhone model, lens info, and exposure settings in place. Sharing with Location turned off in the share sheet does the same. Social apps like Instagram strip GPS on upload but may keep a compressed copy of the rest. Email, iMessage with full-quality images, AirDrop, Discord, Slack, Google Drive, and Dropbox keep everything, including GPS, unless you cleaned the file first.

Common mistakes to avoid. The biggest one is assuming a screenshot is safe. Screenshots do not carry GPS, but they are much lower quality and still contain the date and device info in some cases. Another mistake is converting HEIC to JPG and assuming the location disappeared — conversion usually copies the GPS over. People also forget about Live Photos and videos: a .MOV paired with a Live Photo has its own location track, and iPhone videos store GPS the same way photos do. If you only clean the still image, the video file can still give you away.

A simple routine that works. If you post publicly, turn off Camera location for everyday use and only enable it for trips where you want a location memory. Before sending anything by message, email, or AirDrop, either use Share → Options → Location off, or drop the file into the cleaner below. Check the cleaned copy by swiping up in Photos — if no map appears, the GPS is gone. That thirty-second habit protects your home address, your kids' school, and places you visit often.

Frequently asked questions

Can I remove location from a photo I already sent?
Not from the other person's copy. Once the original file is sent, they already have the coordinates. You can remove the location from your own copy so future shares are safe, and you can ask the recipient to delete the original, but there is no way to pull back data that has already been delivered.

Does taking a screenshot remove GPS?
Yes, a screenshot does not include GPS coordinates, but it is a workaround, not a solution. The image is lower resolution, colours can shift slightly, and you lose the original quality. For one quick share it is fine. For anything you want to keep at full quality, strip the metadata from the original instead.

Does AirDrop remove location data?
No. AirDrop sends the original file with all metadata intact, including GPS, date, and camera info. If you AirDrop a photo to a friend, a client, or a stranger at an event, they can look up exactly where it was taken. Clean the file before you AirDrop it if location matters.
